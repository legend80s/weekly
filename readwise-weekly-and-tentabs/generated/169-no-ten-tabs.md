## 一、Most highlighted Articles of the week

### 1.1 [The components of agent memory](https://anthropic.com/research/components-of-agent-memory/)

![The components of agent memory](https://readwise-assets.s3.amazonaws.com/media/wisereads/articles/the-components-of-agent-memory/cover_image.jpg)

Training an agent to capture memories that prove useful down the line is a thorny problem. Anthropic’s researchers break down the tradeoffs and how the industry borrows from the human brain. "Researchers at Letta and UC Berkeley proposed sleep-time compute as a way for models to use idle time between interactions to reorganize their existing context. The same idea can help agents organize their memory by reflecting on logs to fix mistakes or produce new notes and representations. This process is often called dreaming."

Anthropic · 15 mins | **anthropic.com**

### 1.3 [Writing as a thinking tool](https://muratbuffalo.blogspot.com/2026/11/writing-as-thinking-tool.html)

![Writing as a thinking tool](https://readwise-assets.s3.amazonaws.com/media/wisereads/articles/writing-as-a-thinking-tool/cover_image.png)

MongoDB research scientist Murat Demirbas recalls staring at text on a page in middle school, understanding nothing. Making mind maps on paper made things click for him, and blogging does the same now: "I refer back to my own blog constantly. Because I strained my brain writing those posts, rereading them re-fires the same neurons, and I reconstruct the understanding quickly. In this sense, the blog became my external memory. The thoughts I never wrote down do not stick well, because they were never fully thought."

Murat Demirbas · 6 mins | **muratbuffalo.blogspot.com**


## 二、Most highlighted YouTube Video of the week

### [This Liquid Can Pass Through (Almost) Anything](https://youtube.com/watch/?v=KfODG5REyss)

![This Liquid Can Pass Through (Almost) Anything](https://readwise-assets.s3.amazonaws.com/media/wisereads/articles/this-liquid-can-pass-through-a/cover_image_PKmtRH6.jpg)

Veritasium tracks scientists' race to the coldest temperatures possible, turning gas after gas to liquid. Helium held out longest, and when cooled further still, it became a superfluid that does the (seemingly) impossible. "If you put it in a cup, a thin film climbs up and over the walls, so it empties itself. You can also use superfluid helium to create a fountain with no moving parts. It is powered solely by light. Scientists spent 30 years looking at this liquid without realizing what they had created."

## 五、Hand-picked book of the week

### [Essays](https://readwise.io/reader/fd/520203188)

![Essays](https://readwise-assets.s3.amazonaws.com/media/wisereads/articles/essays/cover_image.jpg)

Emerson left the pulpit in 1832 because he could no longer give communion in good conscience. He lectured for a living after that, and much of the material became two volumes of essays, published in 1841 and 1844. Whitman found his idea of the American poet in them. Nietzsche took the epigraph for The Gay Science from one. Self-Reliance is the best known, and it’s where Emerson defends changing his mind.
"A foolish consistency is the hobgoblin of little minds, adored by little statesmen and philosophers and divines. With consistency a great soul has simply nothing to do. He may as well concern himself with his shadow on the wall. Speak what you think now in hard words and tomorrow speak what tomorrow thinks in hard words again, though it contradict everything you said today... To be great is to be misunderstood."
